title: Hedgehogs and Foxes
---

> The fox knows many things, but the hedgehog knows one big thing.

## Hedgehogs

**Hedgehogs** organize their thoughts and actions around a central, unifying vision or principle.

They interpret the world through this single lens, often at the expense of nuance.

Examples might include:

- Plato, focused on ideals
- Hegel, focused on historical dialectics
- Marx, focused on class struggle

## Foxes

**Foxes** recognize and embrace complexity.

They acknowledge that the world cannot be fully reduced to one idea or system.

They navigate uncertainty and contradiction, often drawing on diverse perspectives.

Thinkers like Aristotle, Shakespeare, and Tolstoy exemplify this category.

## Berlin’s Framework

Berlin’s classification is not meant to be a rigid or definitive categorization of all thinkers.

Rather, it serves as a thought-provoking framework for understanding different approaches to knowledge and interpretation.

It highlights the tension between:

- The desire for a unified, simplified understanding of the world
- The recognition of the world’s inherent complexity and multiplicity of perspectives
